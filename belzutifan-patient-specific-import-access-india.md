## What Is Belzutifan and Who May Require It?

Belzutifan is a prescription medicine that inhibits HIF-2α, a protein involved in the regulation of genes associated with tumour growth and blood vessel formation. In certain cancers, changes in the von Hippel-Lindau pathway can lead to abnormal accumulation of HIF-2α, contributing to tumour development.

Belzutifan is indicated for adults with VHL disease who require treatment for associated renal cell carcinoma, central nervous system haemangioblastomas or pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours, when immediate surgery is not required. It is also indicated for adults with advanced renal cell carcinoma following treatment with a PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitor and a vascular endothelial growth factor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (VEGF-TKI).

The recommended dosage is 120 mg orally once daily, with or without food. Treatment is generally continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, as determined by the treating specialist.

## Treatment Considerations and Medical Monitoring

Belzutifan treatment requires specialist supervision. Two important safety concerns associated with the medicine are anaemia and hypoxia, or reduced oxygen levels in the blood. Haemoglobin levels should be assessed before and during treatment, while patients should be monitored for symptoms and signs of hypoxia. Other reported adverse reactions include fatigue, headache, dizziness, nausea and musculoskeletal pain. The frequency and severity of adverse reactions can differ depending on the patient population and treatment setting.

The prescribing information for Welireg or Belzutifan also includes a warning about embryo-fetal toxicity. Patients should discuss pregnancy-related precautions with their treating physician before starting treatment.

Dose adjustments or treatment interruptions may be necessary depending on adverse reactions and clinical findings. Patients should not change the prescribed dose or discontinue treatment without consulting their oncologist.
