## Understanding Ziihera Dose Modifications and Treatment Interruptions

“Adjusting treatment” can describe several decisions. Your doctor may postpone a dose while a side effect improves, prescribe a smaller dose for future treatment or slow the infusion so that the medicine is delivered over a longer period.

These changes serve different purposes. A slower infusion does not necessarily mean you are receiving less medicine. A temporary interruption also differs from permanent discontinuation, when Ziihera is stopped altogether.

The important starting point is to ask which change your team recommends and what finding led to that decision. The label bases these decisions on the reaction’s severity, its recurrence and the patient’s recovery.

## When Diarrhoea Changes the Next Step

Diarrhoea is a symptom to report promptly during Ziihera treatment. It can lead to dehydration and electrolyte loss, and severe or life-threatening cases can occur.

The treatment regimen matters. When Ziihera is given with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-containing chemotherapy, with or without tislelizumab-jsgr, the label requires preventive loperamide during the first cycle. Patients should follow the prescribed plan and contact their healthcare team immediately about new or worsening diarrhoea.

Your healthcare team determines the severity grade; report symptoms promptly rather than trying to grade them yourself. Whether Ziihera continues depends on the clinical assessment:

- **Mild or moderate diarrhoea:** The label does not require a dose modification, but appropriate treatment and monitoring are still needed.
- **Severe, Grade 3 diarrhoea:** Ziihera is withheld until symptoms improve to Grade 1 or less. Treatment may then resume at the same dose, or a reduction may be considered.
- **Recurrent Grade 3 diarrhoea:** Treatment is withheld and medical management is optimised. Ziihera may resume at a reduced dose once diarrhoea improves to Grade 1 or less.

Permanent discontinuation is required for recurrent Grade 3 diarrhoea lasting more than three days despite optimised management, or for life-threatening Grade 4 diarrhoea.

When several medicines are being used together, the team also evaluates other drugs that may be contributing before modifying Ziihera. This is why reporting symptoms early—and explaining how they have changed—helps the team assess the whole regimen.

## A Heart Test May Prompt a Pause Even Without an Infusion Reaction

Some treatment decisions begin with a test result. Ziihera’s prescribing information includes monitoring of left ventricular ejection fraction, or LVEF, a measure of the heart’s pumping function.

The label recommends withholding Ziihera for at least four weeks if LVEF:

- Falls by **16 percentage points or more** from the pretreatment baseline; or
- Falls **below 50%**, together with a decrease of **at least 10 percentage points** from baseline.

LVEF is reassessed within four weeks. Restarting within four to eight weeks is permitted if it returns to normal limits and the decrease from baseline is no more than 15 percentage points.

The label recommends permanently discontinuing Ziihera if LVEF has not recovered to within 15 percentage points of the pretreatment baseline, or if symptomatic congestive heart failure is confirmed. Your team must interpret the measurements together with your clinical condition; a single number should not be used to make treatment decisions independently.

## An Infusion Reaction May Change the Rate Rather Than the Dose

A reaction during an infusion raises a different question: can the infusion continue safely, and at what rate?

For a **mild reaction**, the label recommends reducing the infusion rate by half. For a **moderate reaction**, the infusion is stopped immediately and appropriate treatment is given. Once symptoms resolve, it may restart at half the previous rate.

A **severe, Grade 3 infusion reaction** requires stopping the infusion immediately and promptly treating the symptoms. The infusion must not restart during that same cycle, even if symptoms completely resolve. Subsequent infusions should be administered at half the previous infusion rate.

Recurrent severe reactions require permanent discontinuation. A life-threatening reaction requires immediate stopping and permanent discontinuation.

Tell the administering team immediately if symptoms develop. Decisions about restarting belong to the healthcare professionals monitoring the reaction.

## What Happens When Another Side Effect Becomes Severe?

The label provides instructions for other adverse reactions as well. Grade 3 reactions generally require withholding Ziihera until improvement to Grade 1 or less. Treatment can resume at the same dose, with a reduction considered if Grade 3 symptoms recur.

Grade 4 reactions generally require permanent discontinuation. There is a specific exception for electrolyte imbalances or laboratory abnormalities corrected within three days of onset: treatment may resume at the same dose after improvement to Grade 1 or less. Recurrent Grade 4 electrolyte imbalances or laboratory abnormalities require permanent discontinuation.

## Restarting Does Not Always Mean Returning to the Original Dose

After recovery, the next question is how treatment will resume. The reduced dose depends on the condition being treated, body weight and the prescribed schedule.

The FDA label recommends permanent discontinuation if the initial dose reduction cannot be tolerated. These are prescribing instructions for the treating team, rather than dose choices for patients to make themselves.
