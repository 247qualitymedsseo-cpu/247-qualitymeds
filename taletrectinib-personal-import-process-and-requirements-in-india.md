## What Is Taletrectinib (Ibtrozi)?

Taletrectinib is an oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets ROS1, a protein involved in signalling pathways that can drive cancer cell growth when altered by gene rearrangements. In ROS1-positive NSCLC, these rearrangements can lead to the formation of abnormal fusion proteins that promote uncontrolled cellular proliferation. Taletrectinib inhibits ROS1 signalling, including activity against certain resistance mutations, and has also demonstrated inhibitory effects on TRKA, TRKB, and TRKC. It is marketed under the brand name Ibtrozi, is a targeted therapy developed for adults with locally advanced or metastatic ROS1-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Treatment selection requires confirmation of ROS1 rearrangements in tumour tissue or other appropriate tumour specimens using a suitable molecular testing method.

The medicine may be considered for patients who have not previously received a ROS1-targeted therapy, as well as those whose disease has progressed after an earlier ROS1 inhibitor. However, suitability depends on the patient's diagnosis, previous treatment history, overall clinical condition, and the treating oncologist's assessment.

Its approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in June 2025 marked an important development in the treatment of this specific form of lung cancer, particularly for patients who need a targeted treatment option after disease progression on earlier therapies.

One clinically important feature of taletrectinib is its activity against the ROS1 G2032R resistance mutation, which can emerge during treatment with earlier ROS1 inhibitors. Clinical studies have also demonstrated responses in patients with central nervous system (CNS) metastases, making its intracranial activity relevant when evaluating treatment options for patients whose cancer has spread to the brain.

## Clinical Evidence and Potential Treatment Benefits

The efficacy of taletrectinib was evaluated in the TRUST-I and TRUST-II clinical trials involving patients with locally advanced or metastatic ROS1-positive NSCLC. The findings indicate that taletrectinib can produce meaningful tumour responses in both treatment-naïve and previously treated patients. Intracranial responses were also observed among patients with measurable CNS metastases in the clinical studies. In a small subgroup of patients whose tumours carried resistance mutations after prior ROS1 inhibitor treatment, responses were observed in patients with the G2032R mutation.

Clinical response rates do not guarantee an individual outcome, and results from separate trial groups should not be interpreted as a direct comparison between treatments. An oncologist must assess the relevance of these findings to each patient's circumstances.

## Understanding the Named Patient Program (NPP)

A Named Patient Program (NPP) is a term commonly used for arrangements that facilitate access to a medicine for an identified patient when it is not routinely available in the patient's country. Such arrangements may involve the treating physician, the medicine manufacturer or an authorised supplier, and a specialist import or access service.

For patients considering taletrectinib in India, a named-patient access arrangement may help coordinate prescription review, overseas supplier enquiries, regulatory documentation, and shipment planning. However, the term NPP does not itself establish legal permission to import a medicine. The actual process must comply with the Indian rules applicable to the medicine, the proposed import, and the intended patient.

Patients should also distinguish between a named-patient import and an Expanded Access Program (EAP). An EAP is generally a manufacturer- or sponsor-supported route for eligible patients who cannot access an investigational or otherwise unavailable treatment through conventional options. Eligibility and availability depend on the specific program. FDA approval of Ibtrozi does not, by itself, establish that a manufacturer-sponsored EAP is open to patients in India.

## Taletrectinib Dosage and Administration

The recommended dosage of Ibtrozi is 600 mg orally once daily on an empty stomach. Patients should avoid food for at least two hours before and two hours after taking the medicine. Capsules should be swallowed whole rather than opened, crushed, chewed, or dissolved. The medicine should be taken at approximately the same time each day and continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, as determined by the treating clinician.

Patients should avoid grapefruit and grapefruit-containing drinks during treatment. The medicine can interact with several other drugs, including strong or moderate CYP3A inhibitors and inducers, as well as medicines that prolong the QTc interval. Proton pump inhibitors and H2 receptor antagonists can also affect taletrectinib exposure. Patients should therefore disclose all prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, and supplements they use before starting treatment. They should not stop or alter another medicine without medical advice.

If a dose is missed or vomiting occurs after a dose, the prescribing information directs patients to take the next dose at its scheduled time on the following day rather than taking an additional dose. Any questions about dosing should be discussed with the treating team.
